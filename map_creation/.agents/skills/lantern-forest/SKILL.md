# Compose woodland

Use the selected project style rather than assuming the original Lantern Sea palette or camera. Read [art direction](../../../docs/art-direction.md) for asset suitability and [review](../../../docs/review.md) for route and catchment checks.

## Habitat before individual trees

Define woodland extent, damp hollows, exposed slopes, glades, paths and settlement exclusions. Weight canopy roles by habitat; do not scatter every species uniformly. Preserve broad transitions and quiet ground. For atlas work, canopy groups are scale cues, not a census of real trees.

Place mature masses first, then younger companions and a bounded amount of edge regeneration. Check actual visual variety: six files can still be six near-identical silhouettes. Track repeated variants and avoid immediate duplicates. Do not mirror or rotate assets with baked directional light.

Use irregular local groups rather than alternating species or rigid bands. Ground edges with short supporting trunks, root contact, understory and shared shade. Put detail in useful gaps and banks; do not bury decoration beneath every crown. A dark oval under each tree is not coherent forest-floor light.

## Camera and integration

Compare candidate trees beside accepted architecture on actual terrain at intended size. Reject side portraits or top-down radial crowns when they contradict the chosen view. Do not stretch sprites to correct perspective. An edge tree needs support, but requesting more exposed bark alone can turn it into a tall frontal portrait.

Keep terrain, floor/contact treatment and crowns separate when assembling assets. Preserve useful silhouette variety during generative finishing; retain the original assembly, since painting can homogenize species, remove trunks and move roads.

A stamp records source IDs/hashes, placement anchors, bounds including shadows and roots, habitat, native size and clearing/edge connections. Export from source masters, never an enlarged screenshot.

## Character and repair

Let mood follow terrain and land use: managed edges, neglected interiors, wet hollows and exposure. Avoid a uniform grey wash or an abrupt political-border-like change in forest color. Preserve focal habitat when softening transitions; a swamp should not become ordinary woodland. Use occasional discoveries where meaningful, not one ruin per clearing. Ordinary groves need no fortress wall without an established purpose.

Keep roads visibly continuous and both riverbanks traceable under canopy. Inspect every repair-mask perimeter: crowns can conceal a seam while closing a path. Trace entire affected journeys and catchments after each change.

Deliver native crops plus a safe context preview, source/stamp recipes where used, observed issues and review status. Technical sharpness, file counts and valid alpha do not establish artistic approval.
