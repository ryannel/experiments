# Compose a settlement

Read [world planning](../../../docs/world-planning.md) for population and territorial assumptions and [review](../../../docs/review.md) for visible connectivity.

## Choose the representation

At atlas scale, a few compact roof masses and a distinctive landmark can stand for an entire settlement. Do not expand every marker into a literal village with all households and production facilities. Compare capitals as label-free thumbnails; a larger grey roof carpet is not visual hierarchy. Keep text in a separate editable layer.

At local scale, begin with why people live here and the trips they make: water, fields, work, fuel, stock, storage and trade. Record household and communal occupancy separately. Shared providers may serve several households; every function does not require another specialist building. Do not infer food capacity from attractive garden area.

## Shared space first

Compose through-routes, useful squares, working yards and connected frontages before choosing roofs. Terrain and established travel shape lanes. Use uneven setbacks and related materials; avoid random orientation or repeated fenced compounds that create isolated properties.

Assign actual door/ground anchors and occupied footprints. Reserve useful cart, foot and stock widths, rear service access and future land before infill. Connect barns to work areas, wells to collection space and inns to passing traffic. A physically non-overlapping building can still pinch a route.

Paint wear that explains use: worn thresholds touching doors, unloading areas beside stores, modest kitchen beds, shared trampled ground and surviving grass. Avoid universal lush estates, identical dirt ovals and decorative clutter. Wealth, maintenance and activity should vary deliberately.

## Assets and finishing

Match the selected camera, light and regional materials. Use distinct compatible sources. Protect roofs and walls during ground integration; broad repainting often softens architecture. False transparency, floating platforms and malformed roofs require replacement or local repair, not more texture.

Keep compound/stamp entrances, footprints and shared edges editable. Closed enclosures are specific functional types, not the default village module. Build a representative house/woodland/road interface before repeating the treatment.

## Structural-purpose review

For a significant structure identify users, purpose, scale and access. A gate must occupy an actual opening; a detached gate beside a continuous wall fails. Trace every fence, doorway approach and through-road, including inherited pens and stores. Inspect river water and both banks through bridges and harbors. Do not invent secret lore to excuse an accidental wall or impossible building.

Growth can include demolition, conversion and collective works. Earlier phase layouts are editable proposals, not sacred history. Update residents, services and routes after changes; keep new providers usable before removing old provision.

Return a population/provision ledger appropriate to the scale, native scene review, exact sources and open issues. Overall visual approval does not certify every doorway, capacity assumption or political relationship.
