# What this experiment taught us

The question was whether a world could be built as both a believable place and a coherent painting, while using AI assistance for art and keeping the process editable.

The strongest results came from a division of work. A structural plan established land, water, routes and required places. Image generation supplied atmosphere, materials and local detail. Inspection compared the painting against the plan. Local masks allowed a good scene to survive repairs.

## What worked

A shared visual direction gave houses, woodland and terrain common light and scale. Small pilots exposed incompatible assets before a large library was built. Settlement design improved when paths, working yards and ordinary households came before decorative objects. Region-by-region painting made native detail possible without pretending that a small image could simply be enlarged into a detailed atlas.

The source project's complete atlas record reports 81 production tiles and a native 12,276 × 8,184 master. This package contains only a 1600 × 1067 visual preview. The final source review records agent inspection of the assembled overview, regional crops, native sites and joins; user review of the finished eastern expansion was still pending. That status is preserved here.

## What failed or needed revision

- More texture did not fix incompatible camera angles or weak large-scale composition.
- Tree variants could still look repetitive; source counts were a poor substitute for looking.
- Repeated enclosed farm compounds made a settlement feel like separate estates.
- Painting could close river mouths, invent forks, move buildings or leave detached gates.
- An attractive bridge crop did not establish that its river or road continued correctly outside the crop.
- More roofs and more pixels did not give a country enough geographic room.
- Whole-scene repainting could soften good architecture and change unrelated features.

## What the public kit adds

The original forest and village instructions accumulated setting-specific paths and history. This kit extracts their reusable decisions and adds a complete path into the work: brief, style comparison, geography and society planning, a representative pilot, expansion, refinement and release.

Its new Python helper is deliberately smaller than the private production pipeline. It demonstrates structural guides, native candidate bookkeeping, masks and explicit review. It neither reconstructs the showcase nor proves unattended map generation. The example world is new, fictional and public-safe.

A useful next experiment is to complete two adjacent painted regions, review their shared river and road, then measure attempts and repair effort. That provides more credible evidence about scaling than either a single beautiful crop or a large synthetic object-count benchmark.
