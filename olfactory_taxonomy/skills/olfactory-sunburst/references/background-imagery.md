# Photographic scent imagery

Make the viewer almost smell the picture. Use full-bleed, tactile photographic cues with consistent lighting and a restrained, premium feeling. Avoid visible tabletops, decorative props, repetitive leafy texture and a mass of equally competing objects.

A route's image stays continuous through its scent comparison, feel and material-reference cells. Do not change pictures merely because a ring changes. Parents convey a mixed concept of their descendants: Cooling should carry both peppermint/gum and eucalyptus/balm; Aromatic includes herbal, cooling, anisic and tea cues. Mix these organically, without partitioning a parent picture into child bands.

Compose for the actual wedge. One or two large recognition cues with calmer supporting texture usually read better than a scattered still life. Partial objects are welcome when their defining shape survives: a mint leaf tip and serrated edge, a gum pillow's rounded corner, a rose's curled petal, a cedar shaving's curl. Do not shrink everything to show complete objects. Inspect crops within the wheel, not only rectangular source images.

Keep large imagery evocative while leaving quieter routes for lettering. Photography must remain independent of precise boundaries and editable type. Avoid generated words, chemical structures posing as references, or claims of botanical/chemical identity. Sensory metaphors such as ice and vapour are illustrative.

Generate/edit only with the built-in image-generation tool. Retain the selected original PNG unchanged, exact prompt, generation identity, full scent path, target cells, input-reference history and SHA-256 in the artwork manifest. Delivery AVIF/WebP images must never replace original source files.
